# Redpen

When writing or reviewing marketing copy, do not begin by generating lines.
Run this editorial loop.

## 1. Find the truth

Build a compact ledger: observed facts, user-provided facts, labeled
inferences, and unknowns. Find the product's real verbs, objects, numbers,
mechanism, constraints, tradeoffs, and proof. Never invent metrics, customer
language, scarcity, or emotional states.

## 2. Enter one reader moment

Locate one scene in one person's day: what just happened, what is in front of
them, what they would mutter, what gets worse, what they distrust, and what
concrete change would make them act.

Emotion is not an adjective. Cause it with scene, stakes, friction, contrast,
or consequence. Do not write "empowering" when the result can be shown.

## 3. Create three territories

Write three different arguments, not synonyms:

1. lived scene;
2. sharp fact or contrast;
3. mechanism or identity.

Delete a territory if it uses the same truth and emotional movement as another.

## 4. Compare before polishing

Judge candidates blind and pairwise from three views: rushed target reader,
skeptical buyer, experienced editor. Evaluate recognition, pull, trust,
emotional movement, ownability, and cadence. Quote the winning fragment and
name the losing defect. "More engaging" is not a verdict.

Preserve the winner's load-bearing fragment. Revise only its largest defect,
then challenge it once more. Maximum three rounds. Keep the original in the
tournament; rewrites often erase its only human phrase.

## 5. Finish with the three questions

Every shipped line must be:

1. visualizable;
2. falsifiable;
3. impossible for a competitor to sign unchanged.

Then check: intended meaning lands in two seconds, facts do the persuading, no
famous line was copied with nouns swapped, and it sounds natural aloud.

## Output

Put finished copy first. Then at most four short lines: reader moment, truth
used, why it won, and a runner-up only when it represents a different
territory. For review, show the smallest useful redline.

Never trade truth, safety, accessibility, or legal accuracy for punch.
