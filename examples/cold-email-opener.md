# How to write a cold email opener that earns the next line

The first line decides whether the rest gets read. "I hope this email finds you well" is a delete trigger. So is any opener that could have been sent to a thousand people.

## Before

> **Hi — I came across your company and was really impressed with what you're doing in your space.**

Can't visualize it, can't prove it, and it's the exact opener sitting in every inbox. Pure wallpaper.

## Observed fact for this example

The recipient's public docs call a feature **instant rollback**. The homepage
buries the same feature under the generic label **deployment controls**.

## After

> **Your docs call it instant rollback. Your homepage makes me hunt for it under deployment controls.**

- **Visualize?** Yes — you see someone moving between the docs and homepage.
- **Falsify?** Yes — both labels are publicly checkable.
- **Nobody else?** Yes — the line points to this recipient's actual language.

## The move

Point, don't talk. Instead of *saying* you're impressed, Redpen points at a
fact the recipient can verify — one that took research, which is exactly why it
earns attention. The observation also gives the recipient something useful:
their strongest product phrase is hidden from buyers.

**Rule of thumb:** open with a fact only you bothered to find. Effort is the message; the fact just carries it.
