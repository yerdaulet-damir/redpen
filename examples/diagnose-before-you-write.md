# Same product, three readers

One truth ledger, one product, three people standing in different places. The
copy changes because the *situation* changes — not because the second draft
found better adjectives.

This is what step 2 of the loop is for. The finishing gates run at the end on
all three versions, and they pass all three. Gates tell you a sentence is
sound. They can't tell you it's aimed at anyone.

## The brief

**Product:** a code review tool.

**Truth ledger:**

- *Observed:* it reads the repo's last 200 merged pull requests before
  reviewing a new one.
- *Provided (user, unverified):* teams report fewer follow-up review rounds.
- *Inferred:* reviewers on the same team enforce conventions inconsistently.
  Plausible, not established — so it can seed a line, but it can't be stated as
  a finding.
- *Unknown:* actual effect on time-to-merge. Nothing may claim it.

## Reader A — three of these already installed, all ignored

**Scene:** a Slack channel where the bot's comments get 👀 and nothing else.
**Inner sentence:** "another one of these."
**Resistance:** they've been burned by generic lint dressed up as review.

Nothing about the outcome will move them; they've heard the outcome and stopped
believing it. What they haven't heard is *how this one is different*.

> **It reads your last 200 merged PRs before it reviews the next one.**

The mechanism is the ownable part — it's the one sentence a rival can't sign
unchanged.

## Reader B — sick of the third round of nitpicks, doesn't know tools like this exist

**Scene:** Friday, second re-request for review, three comments about naming.
**Inner sentence:** "this could have been one round."
**Resistance:** none, really. They just don't know this is buyable.

Here the mechanism is premature — they don't yet want the thing it explains.
State the outcome plainly and get out of the way.

> **The nitpicks are fixed before your reviewer opens the tab.**

## Reader C — doesn't consider review a problem worth solving

**Scene:** a lead who thinks their review culture is fine, because nobody has
complained in the retro.
**Inner sentence:** nothing about review at all.
**Resistance:** they'd read a product claim as a solution to a problem they
don't have.

Price, product, mechanism, and outcome are all dead on arrival. What's left is
recognition — and the only job of the line is to earn the next one.

> **Every reviewer on your team enforces a slightly different rulebook.**

Note this comes from the *inferred* row of the ledger, so the line stays an
observation the reader can test against their own week — not a claim of fact.
And it deliberately doesn't fit every team. A line that everyone nods at is air.

## What actually changed

| | Reader A | Reader B | Reader C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leads with | Mechanism | Outcome | Recognition |
| Because | They've stopped believing outcomes | They've never heard the outcome | They're not in the market yet |
| Visualize / falsify / own | ✅ ✅ ✅ | ✅ ✅ ✅ | ✅ ✅ ✅ |

All three clear the gates. Swap them between readers and two of the three fall
flat — which is the whole point: **the sentence was never the variable.**

## The habit

When a line keeps coming back as "fine but generic," the fix usually isn't in
the words. Ask what the reader already knows, what they've already been
promised by someone else, and what they walked in distrusting. Reading a
handful of competitor pages tends to show you which arguments the market has
stopped hearing — and what nobody is saying yet.
