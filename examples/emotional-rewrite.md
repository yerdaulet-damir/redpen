# From correct copy to felt copy

The three questions are necessary. They are not enough.

## Brief

A tool detects overdue invoices and sends reminders on a schedule chosen by a
freelancer. The freelancer approves the reminder template first.

The reader is a freelance designer reopening the same unpaid invoice on Friday
afternoon.

## Draft that passes the old test

> **Send every overdue invoice reminder on time.**

- **Visual?** Mostly. You can picture a reminder being sent.
- **Falsifiable?** Yes.
- **Ownable?** More specific than generic automation copy.

It is also emotionally dead. It describes software behavior from outside the
reader's life.

## Truth and reader moment

- Observed product action: detects overdue invoices and sends approved
  reminders on a schedule.
- Reader scene: Friday afternoon, invoice open again, deciding whether another
  follow-up will sound desperate.
- Private sentence: "I already did the work. Why am I still asking for the
  money?"
- Movement: awkward and resentful → protected and finished.

## Three territories

### A — scene

> **The invoice tab you reopen every Friday can follow up without you.**

### B — fact

> **Approve the reminder once. It sends when the invoice turns overdue.**

### C — identity

> **Get paid without becoming your own collections department.**

These are not tone variants. A recognizes the recurring moment. B earns trust
through mechanism. C names the role the reader refuses.

## Preference result

**A wins for the hero.** The load-bearing fragment is "the invoice tab you
reopen every Friday." It names a private repeated action without declaring the
reader frustrated. The second half explains the relief.

**B is the subhead.** It answers the skeptical buyer's next question: what is
actually automated, and what remains under my control?

## Final

> **The invoice tab you reopen every Friday can follow up without you.**
>
> Approve the reminder once. It sends on your schedule when an invoice turns
> overdue.

The copy now passes the three questions, but that is not why it works. It works
because a real reader can recognize the hour, the tab, and the conversation
they no longer want to have.
