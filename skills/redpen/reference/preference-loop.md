# Preference loop

Use this reference for important copy, comparisons, or any draft that keeps
becoming cleaner without becoming more alive. The loop borrows the useful
shape of preference optimization: generate distinct candidates, compare them
against an explicit reward model, preserve the winning behavior, and revise
from concrete losses. It is not literal RL and must not be described as model
training.

## Why a checklist is insufficient

A checklist catches violations. It does not reliably choose between two valid
lines, recognize an emotionally inert truth, or preserve the one phrase that
made a draft feel human. Preference comparison supplies contrast. Contrast
makes taste inspectable.

## Candidate contract

Produce three territories, not three rewrites:

- **A — scene:** a lived moment or visible use.
- **B — proof:** a fact, contrast, result, or constraint.
- **C — mechanism/identity:** the different reason it works or the role it
  lets the reader enact.

Every candidate records:

- truth source;
- intended reader movement;
- strongest fragment;
- main risk.

If candidates share the same truth source and emotional movement, they are one
territory wearing three shirts. Regenerate before judging.

## Blind pairwise matches

Hide territory names and compare as A/B. Reverse the order once when stakes are
high; position bias is real. Run:

1. candidate 1 vs candidate 2;
2. winner vs candidate 3;
3. winner vs original;
4. revised winner vs previous winner.

The original remains in the tournament because rewrites often sand away the
only natural phrase in the source.

## Three reader views

### Rushed target reader

- What do I think this is after two seconds?
- Which concrete word remains ten seconds later?
- What question makes me continue?
- Does this sound addressed to my situation or to a market segment?

### Skeptical buyer

- Which claim would I challenge first?
- What evidence or mechanism earns it?
- Where does the copy overstate certainty?
- Does it respect the cost, risk, or work involved?

### Experienced editor

- What is the load-bearing fragment?
- Which words are interchangeable with category language?
- Is the rhythm discovered or manufactured?
- Does the structure echo a famous campaign or common AI template?
- Can one deletion make it stronger without changing its truth?

## Reward dimensions

Score 0–3. Scores are diagnostic, not arithmetic truth.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Recognition | Generic audience | Relevant category | Familiar situation | Private truth named |
| Pull | Complete/dead | Mild interest | Clear unresolved tension | Next line feels necessary |
| Trust | Unsupported | Plausible | Supported | Proof/mechanism carries claim |
| Emotional movement | None | Emotion named | State implied | State changes through scene/stakes |
| Ownability | Anyone | Few rivals | Product-specific | Inseparable from this product |
| Cadence | Generated | Correct | Natural | Conviction with memorable rhythm |

Hard gates override total score:

- invented or upgraded facts;
- false customer voice;
- unclear meaning after two seconds;
- required legal/safety meaning removed;
- famous line or competitor structure copied;
- emotional manipulation unsupported by the situation.

## Valid verdict format

```text
Winner: B
Winning fragment: "the invoice tab you reopen every Friday"
Why it wins: names the reader's repeated action; A only names the category.
Largest defect: the payoff overpromises time saved without evidence.
Revision instruction: keep the fragment; replace the payoff with the verified
Friday reconciliation result.
```

Invalid: "B is more engaging and concise."

## Revision discipline

Do not merge all high-scoring parts. That produces committee copy.

1. Freeze the winner's load-bearing fragment.
2. Name its single largest defect.
3. Change only what answers that defect.
4. Add one challenger from a genuinely different territory.
5. Re-run the match.

Maximum three rounds. More rounds usually optimize for the evaluator and drain
the line of surprise.

## Stop condition

Ship when all are true:

- one candidate wins at least two reader views;
- every claim maps to observed or provided truth;
- its intended emotional movement is caused rather than named;
- no removable word remains;
- the source no longer beats it for naturalness;
- one reader action or next thought is obvious.

If no candidate clears the hard gates, return to evidence or reader scene. Do
not compensate with more language.
