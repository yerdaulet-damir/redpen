# Reader and emotion model

Use this reference when copy is technically clear but emotionally flat, when
the brief contains broad personas instead of lived situations, or when the
request explicitly asks for human, resonant, moving, funny, or memorable copy.

## Emotion is movement

Copy does not need to sound emotional. It needs to move the reader between two
states. Name the transition before writing:

| From | To | Material that can cause it |
|---|---|---|
| Confused | Oriented | A named mechanism, map, or first step |
| Exposed | Protected | A boundary, guarantee, audit trail, or control |
| Behind | Capable | A finite task, visible progress, proof from a peer |
| Skeptical | Curious | A surprising fact that survives scrutiny |
| Resigned | Hopeful | A limitation removed without pretending the work is easy |
| Overwhelmed | Relieved | Fewer decisions, one screen, one action, one owner |
| Invisible | Recognized | Their private situation stated accurately |
| Restless | Committed | A belief, enemy, or choice with consequences |

Avoid emotion labels. A line such as "finally feel empowered" names the desired
state without earning it. Show the condition that produces it.

## The reader scene

Build one compact scene from evidence:

1. **Moment** — when does the problem become impossible to ignore?
2. **Object** — what physical or digital thing are they looking at?
3. **Action** — what are they doing for the third, fifth, or twentieth time?
4. **Consequence** — what is late, lost, awkward, expensive, or publicly seen?
5. **Private sentence** — what do they tell themselves but not the vendor?

Example:

- Persona abstraction: "busy founders need streamlined communication."
- Scene: "It is 11:48 p.m. The launch email is still a blank draft. Slack has
  three versions of the positioning and nobody has chosen one."
- Private sentence: "I know this product. Why can't I explain it?"

The scene gives the copy objects, tension, and voice without adding adjectives.

## Five forms of emotional truth

### Recognition

The reader sees a situation they thought was private. Precision matters more
than intensity. One accurate inconvenience can outperform a dramatic pain
claim.

### Stakes

Show what changes if the problem persists. Stakes may be money, time, status,
trust, safety, identity, or momentum. Do not inflate them. A delayed reply can
matter more than an invented existential crisis.

### Friction

Name the repeated action, compromise, workaround, or social awkwardness. The
best friction is easy to verify and hard for the reader to unsee afterward.

### Contrast

Put two realities close enough that the gap creates feeling: before/after,
expected/actual, competitor/product, public/private, fast/slow, promise/proof.
Do not explain the emotion produced by the contrast.

### Identity

People want outcomes and roles. Show what the action says about them: careful
operator, independent builder, trusted teammate, present parent, serious
craftsperson. Identity must emerge from behavior; never flatter directly.

## Voice without cosplay

Human voice comes from selection and conviction, not decorative informality.

- Use contractions when the speaker would.
- Keep domain words the reader uses.
- Vary sentence length when thought or tension changes.
- Let a strong sentence stand alone.
- Use fragments only when a person would naturally stop there.
- Prefer one strange-but-true detail to manufactured quirkiness.

Do not add fake anecdotes, chatty filler, forced slang, deliberate typos,
random profanity, em-dash confetti, or "Here's the thing" openers to imitate a
human.

## The private-language test

Ask: could the reader have said this exact sentence to a colleague or to
themselves before discovering the product? If yes, the copy may contain their
language. If it sounds like something only a vendor says, keep digging.

## Emotional failure modes

- **Pain theater** — turning an annoyance into catastrophe.
- **Empathy claim** — saying "we understand" without demonstrating it.
- **Generic aspiration** — "do your best work", "unlock your potential".
- **Manufactured intimacy** — pretending friendship or shared experience.
- **Catharsis without mechanism** — promising relief with no reason to believe.
- **Feature emotion** — attaching "delightful" or "effortless" to a feature.
- **Universal reader** — trying to make every person feel seen and seeing none.

## Practical rewrite sequence

When a line feels bloodless:

1. Circle the abstract outcome.
2. Ask when the reader notices its absence.
3. Name the object or action in that moment.
4. Add the smallest true consequence.
5. Remove the emotion word.
6. Read the line aloud from the reader's position.

The goal is not more emotion. It is more reality per word.
